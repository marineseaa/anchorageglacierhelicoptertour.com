## The problem the helicopter solves

Sled dogs cannot run on gravel in July, and there is no snow at road level in southcentral Alaska between May and October. But there is snow — permanent, all-season snow — on the glaciers and snowfields a few thousand feet above the valleys, and kennels move camps up there for the summer so their teams can keep training.

You cannot walk to those camps in a morning and you cannot drive to them at all. Every summer glacier dogsled tour in Alaska is therefore a helicopter tour first, and the aircraft is the largest single line in the price.

## Three tours, two directions

**[Anchorage Area: Helicopter and Glacier Dogsled Tour](/tours/anchorage-area-helicopter-and-glacier-dogsled-tour/)** — the one with the most sled time on this site. Thirty minutes of flying from the Knik River valley over Knik Glacier and Lake George, then **a full hour** with the team on the snow. Most glacier dogsled products in Alaska allocate fifteen to twenty minutes; this gives sixty, which is the difference between a lap and a run. Overboots are provided. From $816, 63 reviews at 4.93. Book through {{< gyg-link tour="147273" >}}Anchorage Area: Helicopter and Glacier Dogsled Tour{{< /gyg-link >}}.

**[Anchorage: Helicopter Glacier Dogsledding Tour](/tours/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding-tour/)** — the southern option, flying from the Alpine Air hangar at Girdwood Airport up to **Punchbowl Glacier at around 3,200 feet**. The camp there belongs to Mitch Seavey's IdidaRide kennel; Seavey has won the Iditarod three times. Roughly 85 minutes on the snow out of a 90-minute glacier experience, outer layers and boots provided, groups capped at ten. From $769. See {{< gyg-link tour="1079145" >}}Anchorage: Helicopter Glacier Dogsledding Tour{{< /gyg-link >}}.

**Palmer: "Dogs and Glaciers"** — a four-hour combination from Outbound Heli Adventures that flies down the Knik River valley to a kennel at Big Lake, sleds there, then continues to Knik Glacier for a landing and a walk on the ice. It is the newest of the three and carries no reviews yet, so we have not given it a page — but it is bookable through {{< gyg-link tour="490542" >}}Palmer: Dogs and Glaciers Sledding and Helicopter Tour{{< /gyg-link >}} if the two-in-one shape appeals.

## Which one, honestly

**Take the Knik valley tour if the dogs are the point.** An hour with a team is a genuinely different experience from twenty minutes, and it is the reason that tour costs more than a straight landing flight.

**Take the Girdwood tour if the drive decides it.** Girdwood is 40 miles and about 45 minutes south of Anchorage down the Seward Highway; the Knik valley is an hour north up the Old Glenn. If your day is already pointing at Turnagain Arm, Portage or Alyeska, the southern tour fits the itinerary and the northern one does not.

**Take the Girdwood tour if you have young children.** Outer layers and boots are provided at the hangar, and the operator follows FAA guidance allowing infants under 24 months to fly on a parent's lap, with a seat purchased for any child aged two or over.

One practical warning on the Girdwood tour: a sled run needs **four people to launch**. Parties of two or three may be moved to another departure to make up a group, and solo travellers should call the operator to be added to an existing booking rather than trying to book online.

## What it costs, and why

$769 to $816 for the two tours with pages here, against $520 for the cheapest straight glacier landing. The gap is the camp: mushers and dogs living on a glacier for a season, flown in, supplied and flown out. Our [cost guide](/guides/how-much-does-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour-cost/) sets the price bands out in full.

If what you actually want is the ice rather than the dogs, the [Knik landing tours](/knik-glacier-helicopter-tours/) start $250 lower and give you longer on the glacier surface. And if you are weighing the two glaciers against each other, the [Knik versus Punchbowl comparison](/guides/knik-glacier-vs-punchbowl-glacier-helicopter-tours/) is the page for that.
