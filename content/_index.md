Search for a glacier helicopter tour in Anchorage and you will find nine of them, all titled *Anchorage: something*. None of them lifts off from Anchorage. The city has no tour heliport; what it has is two clusters of operators an hour away in opposite directions, and the drive is yours to make.

That single fact settles more of the decision than price does, so start there.

## North to the Knik River, or south to Girdwood

**North** — about an hour up the Old Glenn Highway to the Knik River valley near Palmer. Three operators fly from here: Alaska Helicopter Tours out of Alaska Glacier Lodge at 29979 East Knik River Road and out of Knik River Lodge, and Outbound Heli Adventures from its own pad at the end of Lindsey Circle. Every one of them flies to **Knik Glacier** — roughly twenty-five miles long and over five miles across at its widest, one of the largest glaciers in southcentral Alaska, feeding the Knik River down into Knik Arm.

**South** — forty miles down the Seward Highway to Girdwood, about forty-five minutes. Alpine Air Alaska flies from the hangar at Girdwood Airport, 599 Mt. Hood Drive, up into the Chugach to **Punchbowl Glacier**, a snowfield sitting at around 3,200 feet.

The two directions give you different products, not different versions of the same one. Knik is a valley glacier you land *on*: bare, crevassed ice with turquoise meltwater pools opening across it through summer, at low elevation and close enough to the river that the flight in is a valley flight. Punchbowl is a high snowfield, white year-round, which is precisely why a dog team can live there through July.

If you are picking on scenery alone, Knik has the better ice and the better melt pools. If you are picking on schedule, Girdwood is the shorter drive and sits on one of the best road trips in the state. If you are picking on weather — see below.

## What you are actually buying: minutes on the ice

Flight time is what operators advertise. Time on the glacier is what people remember, and the two do not track each other.

The [Palmer departure](/tours/from-palmer-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour/) is the cheapest tour on this site at $520 and gives you **forty-five minutes** on Knik Glacier with microspikes handed out — more ice time than tours costing half as much again. The [flagship landing tour](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landing/), at $522 and rated 4.99 across 85 reviews, gives you thirty minutes on the ice inside a one-hour flight. The [90-minute Girdwood flight](/tours/anchorage-90-min-flight-with-2-landings/) is the longest in the air at $844 and gives you around thirty minutes on the glacier, but adds a second landing on an alpine ridge or a remote mountain lake — a completely different view, and arguably the better photograph.

So: if the ice is the point, the cheapest tour is also one of the best. If the flying is the point, pay for airtime. Our [decision guide](/guides/which-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour-is-right-for-you/) works through it properly, and the [cost breakdown](/guides/how-much-does-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour-cost/) explains why the range runs from $520 to $1,416.

## Weather is the real variable, and it is not evenly distributed

Helicopters need cloud ceiling and visibility. In southcentral Alaska both build through the day: mornings are clearer, and afternoon convective cloud is a standing feature rather than bad luck. Book the earliest slot you can, and book it early in your trip so there is room to be rescheduled.

The operators flying from the Knik River valley argue that their inland position gives them a better dispatch rate than glacier flights working out of Girdwood, Seward, Juneau, Haines or Skagway. It is a marketing claim rather than a published figure, and we have treated it as one — but the underlying geography is real. Turnagain Arm pulls in coastal cloud that the Knik valley, sitting further inland behind a ridge, does not always get. If your trip has one flyable window in it, north is the safer bet.

Every tour listed here carries free cancellation up to 24 hours before departure, and operators reschedule or refund rather than fly into weather. Our [best-time guide](/guides/best-time-for-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour/) goes month by month.

## Weight limits are real, and they are published

This is the part of a helicopter booking that catches people out. Outbound Heli Adventures publishes a **300 lb individual seat limit**, a combined passenger limit of 650 lbs in its three-seat helicopter and 900 lbs in the four-seat one, and a **$350 surcharge** if a party has to be split across two aircraft. Alpine Air notes that a surcharge may apply above 250 lbs. Every operator weighs passengers before boarding, because weight decides seating and fuel load.

Declare weights honestly at booking. This is a flight-safety calculation, not a formality, and a party discovering the problem at check-in has a much worse morning than one that sorted it in advance.

## When the glacier is the setting, not the destination

Four tours use the helicopter to reach something you then do for hours.

The [glacier dogsled tour](/tours/anchorage-area-helicopter-and-glacier-dogsled-tour/) gives a full hour with the team on a snowfield above the Knik — three to four times the sled time most helicopter dogsled products in Alaska allocate. Its [Girdwood equivalent](/tours/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding-tour/) lands on Punchbowl and puts you with Mitch Seavey's IdidaRide kennel; Seavey is a three-time Iditarod champion, and the dogs are there for summer conditioning rather than for show. Both are covered on the [glacier dogsledding page](/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding/).

The [paddleboarding tour](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-and-paddleboarding-tour/) puts you in a dry suit on a turquoise meltwater pool on the glacier surface, with guides carrying bear-safety and medical rescue gear, for four hours. The [ice climbing tour](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-and-ice-climbing-tour/) gives you up to four hours on the ice with an instructor and all the technical gear, and adds ice cave exploration in winter — one of the few things on this site that is genuinely better in the cold months.

Those four cost between $769 and $1,416. That is what a guided half-day with a helicopter commute costs, and it is a different purchase from a flightseeing trip.

## What none of them include

Transport from Anchorage. Every tour on this site excludes it, and most sell it separately if you call after booking — Alaska Helicopter Tours takes transfer requests on 907-272-7777, and Alpine Air arranges Girdwood shuttles for an extra fee. Most guests drive, and both approaches are worth driving: the Old Glenn Highway up the Knik River, and the Seward Highway along Turnagain Arm.

Food is excluded on the short tours and included on the long ones. Gratuities are excluded everywhere. And nothing includes the weather — which is the one thing worth building your itinerary around.
