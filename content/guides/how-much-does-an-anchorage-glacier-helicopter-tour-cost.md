## The three price bands

**$520 – $560: a glacier landing, and nothing else.** One operator sits here. The [Palmer flight](/tours/from-palmer-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour/) is $520 for a 40 to 45-minute flight and forty-five minutes on Knik Glacier with microspikes. The [flagship landing tour](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landing/) is $522 for a one-hour flight with thirty minutes on the ice. These two are the reason nobody should assume a glacier landing is out of reach.

**$760 – $850: more airtime, more landings, or a dog camp.** The [Grand tour](/tours/anchorage-grand-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landings/) is $762 for two hours and three touchdowns. The [heli-hiking day](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-and-hiking-adventure-tour/) is $771 for four hours with a guide. The [Girdwood dogsledding tour](/tours/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding-tour/) is $769 and the [90-minute two-landing flight](/tours/anchorage-90-min-flight-with-2-landings/) is $844. The [Knik valley dogsled tour](/tours/anchorage-area-helicopter-and-glacier-dogsled-tour/) is $816.

**$1,416: a technical half-day.** The [paddleboarding](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-and-paddleboarding-tour/) and [ice-climbing](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-and-ice-climbing-tour/) tours both sit here. Four hours, an instructor or guide team, a full kit of technical equipment, and lunch.

## Why the cheapest tour gives the most ice time

This is the single most counter-intuitive thing about the pricing, and it is worth stating plainly: **the $520 tour gives you forty-five minutes on the glacier and the $844 tour gives you around thirty.**

Helicopter time is expensive; standing on a glacier is not. Once the aircraft is down and the rotor is off, extra minutes on the ice cost the operator almost nothing but a longer slot. So a tour built around a single landing can be generous with ice time and cheap, while a tour built around ninety minutes of flying and two landings costs more and gives you less time out of the aircraft.

If what you want is to be *on* a glacier, buy the cheap one. If what you want is to *fly*, buy the airtime.

## Why helicopter sightseeing costs this much in the first place

A bus can carry dozens of passengers; the largest aircraft on this site carries a handful. That single fact explains most of the price gap between a helicopter tour and any other Anchorage-area excursion. Every flight involves aviation fuel, aircraft maintenance, trained pilots, insurance and specialised landing capability, and the aircraft is expensive whether it's carrying three passengers or six. On top of that, you're paying for access — much of what you see, and everywhere you land, would be difficult or impossible to reach on an ordinary sightseeing day. Judging a helicopter tour purely on cost per hour misses that part of what the price buys.

## How to compare two tours that look similar

Two listings that both say "glacier landing" can differ by an hour of flying, two extra touchdowns, a guide, a lunch and a full set of technical equipment. Before comparing headline prices, check:

| Compare | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Price per person** | The starting point, and the only figure most people check. |
| **Actual flight time vs. total duration** | A 60-minute tour can mean 30 minutes flying and 30 on the ice, or nearly the full hour in the air. They're different products. |
| **Number of landings** | The [Grand tour](/tours/anchorage-grand-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landings/) touches down three times for $762; the flagship tour touches down once for $522. |
| **Glacier landing included?** | Never assume it from the word "glacier" in the title. |
| **Activity included?** | Dog sledding, hiking, paddleboarding and ice climbing explain most of the jump above $760. |
| **Departure point** | Every tour here excludes transport from Anchorage — see below. |
| **Weight policy** | Limits and the $350 second-aircraft charge vary by operator. |
| **Cancellation window** | 24 hours on most tours; 7 days on the Palmer flight specifically. |

## How much should you budget?

Figures below are per person unless marked otherwise, based on this site's current listed prices.

**Solo traveller:** budget around **$520** for the cheapest landing tour, the [Palmer flight](/tours/from-palmer-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour/).

**Couple:** the two cheapest tours on this site both land a couple close to **$1,040–$1,044** — see the [full breakdown for two](/guides/helicopter-tour-for-two-in-alaska/), including what "two tickets" does and doesn't get you in terms of privacy.

**Couple wanting a longer, multi-landing flight:** budget roughly **$1,524** for the [Grand Knik tour](/tours/anchorage-grand-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landings/) at $762 each.

**Couple wanting helicopter dog sledding:** roughly **$1,538–$1,632**, depending on whether you fly from [Girdwood](/tours/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding-tour/) ($769pp) or the [Knik valley](/tours/anchorage-area-helicopter-and-glacier-dogsled-tour/) ($816pp).

**Family of four on the flagship landing tour:** budget from **$2,088** before checking whether every child needs a full seat — see below.

## What is never included

**Transport from Anchorage.** Every tour on this site excludes it. Alaska Helicopter Tours sells transfers if you call 907-272-7777 after booking; Alpine Air arranges a Girdwood shuttle for an extra fee. If you are driving, budget an hour each way north or 45 minutes each way south, plus fuel and — if you do not have a car — a rental for the day.

**Gratuities.** Excluded on every tour. Alpine Air's listing puts it well: appreciated, never expected.

**Food, on the short tours.** Lunch is included on the paddleboarding and ice-climbing days and on nothing else. The heli-hiking tour includes snacks and water but not a meal.

**Warm clothing, on the ice-climbing tour specifically.** That listing excludes warm and waterproof clothing in writing, while providing every piece of technical gear. Two of the other tours provide overboots or full outer layers. See the [what to wear guide](/guides/what-to-wear-on-an-alaska-glacier-helicopter-tour/).

## The surcharges nobody budgets for

**The $350 second-aircraft charge.** Outbound Heli Adventures publishes a 300 lb individual seat limit, and combined passenger caps of 650 lbs in its three-seat helicopter and 900 lbs in the four-seat one. A party that exceeds an aircraft's total and has to be flown in two helicopters pays an additional $350. For a family of four this is a real risk, and it is a number worth checking against your actual weights before you book.

**A possible over-250 lb surcharge at Alpine Air**, applied for safety and comfort. The listing does not publish the amount.

**A child's seat from age two.** Per FAA guidance, one operator allows infants under 24 months to fly on a parent's lap, but a child aged two or over must have a seat purchased. That turns a family of four with a toddler into four full fares.

## Is it worth it?

That depends what you are comparing it to. A [glacier cruise or road-accessible glacier day trip](/) from Anchorage runs roughly $115 to $370 and takes eight to eleven hours, most of it moving. A helicopter landing costs three to five times that and takes three to five hours door to door — but it is the only option that puts you on ice you could not otherwise reach in a morning, and it is the only one where the journey is the view.

Per hour of your holiday, the numbers are closer than they look. Per dollar, they are not. Decide which currency you are spending.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much is a helicopter ride in Alaska?

Near Anchorage, $520 to $1,416 per person for a glacier tour, depending on flight length and whether an activity is attached. A straight landing flight is $520 to $844; a guided half-day with dogsledding, paddleboarding or ice climbing is $769 to $1,416. Prices elsewhere in Alaska — Juneau, Seward, Denali — sit in similar bands.

### Why do prices vary so much between similar-sounding tours?

Airtime and what happens on the ground. Two tours that both say "glacier landing" can differ by an hour of flying, two extra touchdowns, a guide, a lunch and a full set of equipment. Read the flight length and the minutes on the glacier rather than the title.

### What is the cheapest way to land on a glacier near Anchorage?

The [Palmer flight](/tours/from-palmer-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour/) at $520, which is also the tour that gives the most time on the ice — forty-five minutes, with microspikes provided. Groups are capped at seven.

### Are there any hidden costs?

Three worth planning for: transport from Anchorage (never included), a $350 charge at one operator if your party's combined weight requires a second helicopter, and a purchased seat for any child aged two or over. Gratuities are excluded everywhere and left to you.

### Do I pay more in peak season?

The listed starting prices on this site are the operators' own "from" figures and do not swing by season the way hotel rates do, but availability does — July slots on the best-reviewed tours go early. What changes with the season is not the price but the chance of getting the morning you want, which is covered in the [best-time guide](/guides/best-time-for-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour/).

### Is the most expensive tour the best one?

No. The best-value tour depends on what you actually want. Someone taking their first helicopter flight and wanting to stand on a glacier is best served by the $520–$522 landing tours. A photographer chasing maximum aerial scenery is better matched to the $762 [Grand tour](/tours/anchorage-grand-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landings/) and its three landings. Someone who has always wanted to go dog sledding is better matched to the $769–$816 dog-sled tours. The most expensive tour on this site ($1,416) buys a four-hour guided activity, not a better version of the basic flight.

### How much does a helicopter tour for two cost?

The two cheapest tours on this site put a couple at $1,040–$1,044 total. Our [full breakdown for two](/guides/helicopter-tour-for-two-in-alaska/) covers pricing across every tour on this site, plus what "private" does and doesn't mean when two tickets are booked together.
