Yes, you can visit a glacier near Anchorage in November, December, January and February. Most visitors pick one of two trips: a helicopter flight to Knik Glacier with a landing, or a guided walk on Matanuska Glacier, which you reach by road. The year-round {{< gyg-link tour="147264" >}}Knik Glacier Landing{{< /gyg-link >}} with Alaska Helicopter Tours is the quickest way onto the ice; check your date below.

Decide first what *visit* means to you. Some travelers want to stand on glacier ice, some want to see the ice from a helicopter, and others are happy with mountain-and-glacier scenery from a safe viewpoint. In winter Alaska those are three different trips.

1. **Fly to Knik Glacier and land with a helicopter operator based near Palmer.** You get the aerial view and a short stop on or beside the ice. The standard Knik flights are advertised year-round.
2. **Book a guided walk on Matanuska Glacier, off the Glenn Highway.** Choose this if you want more time walking on the ice and would like to skip the helicopter. Guided winter tours leave from Anchorage with hotel pickup.

You can also drive into Portage Valley south of Anchorage, or look for a legal roadside view of Matanuska Glacier. A scenic drive is not permission to walk onto the ice on your own, and many summer trails, viewpoints and glacier trips close or turn hazardous in winter.

> **At a glance:** Book Knik by helicopter for flightseeing and a landing, Matanuska with a guide for a longer walk on the ice, and a roadside viewpoint only when distant scenery is enough. ### Is the helicopter worth it if you don't want to hike?

Usually yes, if you enjoy flightseeing and the budget works. The helicopter reaches remote ice and snowfields without a long approach on foot, and you see the width of the glacier, the terrain around it and the mountains behind it in a way no roadside stop shows.

The trade-off is time on the ice. The shorter Knik tours are flights with a landing; they are not long guided hikes. If you want two or three hours exploring ice formations on foot, the guided Matanuska walk below suits you better.

## Option 3: a guided Matanuska Glacier hike, the way to walk on ice without flying

Matanuska Glacier lies about 100 miles northeast of Anchorage along the Glenn Highway. You reach it by road, through a private glacier access area, with no aircraft needed. The glacier is not open for casual entry on your own from the public recreation site; visitors go onto the ice on a guided excursion.

Book a Matanuska walk in winter if:

- You want to spend real time on the glacier itself instead of in the air.
- You are comfortable with an active outdoor walk in winter clothing and traction gear.
- You'd like one guided full-day trip with transport instead of renting a car and arranging access.
- Your budget is closer to a group land tour than a helicopter fare.

Matanuska walks can reach sculpted ice, ridges and blue-ice sections where the guide judges access safe. No formation, cave, route or color is guaranteed: the guide picks a path for that day's ice and snow.

### Can you drive to Matanuska Glacier yourself in winter?

You may be able to drive to the glacier access meeting point on the Glenn Highway when the roads allow and join a guide there. That is different from driving onto the glacier. Confirm the meeting instructions, and whether the private access road is operating, before you set off.

If you haven't driven in an Alaska winter, take a tour with Anchorage pickup. The drive is scenic but long enough for darkness, icy roads, drifting snow and changing weather to matter. Allow more time than a summer map estimate and check [Alaska 511 road conditions](https://511.alaska.gov/) before you leave.

### Is Matanuska easier or harder than a helicopter landing?

Usually harder on the body. A guided winter walk on Matanuska means hours outdoors on uneven frozen ground, with the boots, helmets and crampons or ice cleats the operator supplies. A short helicopter landing needs much less walking, though you still step in and out of the aircraft and move on snowy or icy ground.

Pick by the day you want. If 15 to 30 minutes near the ice would disappoint you, book a proper guided walk. If you dream of the flight over the glacier with a short photo stop, book the helicopter.

## Option 4: Matanuska Glacier from the Glenn Highway, a view and no ice walk

You don't need a helicopter just to see a glacier. Matanuska Glacier is visible from the Glenn Highway, with dedicated viewpoints in the months when they are open.

Matanuska Glacier State Recreation Site sits at Mile 101 of the Glenn Highway. When it is open, its parking area and the Edge Nature Trail, a 20-minute walk through the forest, lead to glacier viewing platforms. Alaska State Parks says the site stays open until winter snow and ice close it, and Travel Alaska lists it as closed in winter.

A summer guide's easy 20-minute viewing trail is not a winter access promise. You may still see the glacier from the highway or a plowed, designated pullout, but:

- Never stop in the travel lane or on an unplowed shoulder.
- Don't assume a summer turnout is cleared in winter.
- Don't enter a closed state recreation area or a private road.
- Don't climb over roadside snowbanks into unknown terrain.
- Don't try to reach the ice on your own from a highway viewpoint.

This option works when you have a winter-ready vehicle, the weather and road reports look good and a wide landscape photo is enough. It does not replace an organized winter glacier trip.

For site details, check [Alaska State Parks' Matanuska Glacier State Recreation Site page](https://dnr.alaska.gov/parks/aspunits/matsu/matsuglsrs.htm) and [Travel Alaska's Matanuska Glacier guide](https://www.travelalaska.com/destinations/parks-public-lands/matanuska-glacier).

## Option 5: Portage Valley, winter scenery but no easy glacier walk

Portage Valley, south of Anchorage off the Seward Highway, is good for snowy mountains, valley views and winter photography. It is much closer to the city than Matanuska Glacier, but several common assumptions about Portage are wrong in winter.

### Can you see Portage Glacier from the Begich, Boggs Visitor Center?

Not from the visitor center's main deck. Portage Glacier has retreated out of sight of it. The lake and mountains still look impressive, but if you come for a close view of the glacier itself you may leave disappointed.

The visitor center opens mainly in summer. Some older brochures mention limited winter opening and other official material lists it as closed, so don't plan a winter trip around it. Phone the Glacier Ranger District or check current Forest Service notices first.

### What about Byron Glacier Trail?

Summer articles often suggest it as a short glacier-area hike. The U.S. Forest Service warns that winter travel on Byron Glacier Trail is unsafe because of avalanche danger: the valley is narrow, with steep snow-loaded slopes overhead, and snow caves and unstable ice add to the risk.

Don't use Byron Glacier Trail as a casual winter stand-in for a guided glacier tour, and don't cross frozen Portage Lake to reach the ice because a social media video made it look easy. Lake ice, avalanche exposure and access need an expert local assessment.

In Portage, enjoy the scenery from the road when conditions allow and stay out of avalanche terrain. For a guided walk on the ice, go to Matanuska; for a remote glacier landing, fly to Knik.

See [Visit Anchorage's Portage Valley overview](https://www.anchorage.net/outdoors/glaciers/portage-valley/) and the Forest Service's [Byron Glacier Trail safety information](https://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/fseprd587202.pdf).

## What is glacier access like from November to February?

Snow depth, temperature, daylight and the list of specialist tours change across these four months.

### November: early winter, changing access

The year-round Knik sightseeing flights run in November, and guided Matanuska walks operate in winter too. Winter-only activities start later: an ice cave or ice-climbing tour may not have opened by mid-November.

November brings shrinking daylight and a fast-changing snowpack. If you are driving yourself, stick to open roads and a guided trip, and skip improvised glacier access.

### December: the shortest days

December is the month when timing matters most. In mid-December Anchorage gets roughly five and a half hours of daylight. A long drive and a glacier walk can fill most of it, so road tours start early. A midday helicopter slot gives better light for photos than an early or late flight.

Some winter exploration and ice cave tours begin in December, on different opening dates. Check the season of the tour you want, not just the operator's general "year-round" label.

### January: midwinter glacier trips

January suits guided winter glacier trips, with snow and ice formations as the main sight. Expect hard cold and the chance of flight or road disruption. Keep a spare day in your plans and don't put a glacier trip just before a non-refundable flight home.

### February: more daylight, the same winter conditions

By mid-February Anchorage has around nine hours of daylight, so longer trips and the drive back are easier to fit in. You still need proper clothing, experienced guides and flexibility, and photographers get more choice of departure times and light.

In any month, a published winter season is not the same as a seat on your date. A tour can have limited departures, a minimum number of guests or a weather cancellation.

## Winter safety before you go onto the ice

A glacier can look peaceful under snow, but a white surface can hide crevasses, meltwater channels or weak ice. Cold weather does not make every route safe. Go with a properly equipped guide or operator who picks the route for the day's conditions.

### Don't walk onto glacier ice on your own

Glacier ice is not a public footpath. Snow can bridge crevasses, hide holes or disguise weak edges. Another group's photo from a spot does not mean it is safe to reach this week.

### Don't treat ice caves as guaranteed or safe

Caves and ice arches change and can collapse. Guided operators visit a suitable formation when conditions allow, but an ice cave photo is no promise that you will go inside one. Never enter an ice cave nobody has assessed, or walk under unstable ice and avalanche slopes.

For cave tours and their safety rules, see the [ice cave section of our winter tours guide](/guides/winter-glacier-helicopter-tours-from-anchorage/#are-glacier-ice-caves-safe-to-visit).

### Weather affects aircraft and roads differently

Helicopters need good visibility, light enough wind, high enough cloud and a safe landing site. Road tours depend on the highway as well as the ice. A day can be flyable but icy on the road, or fine for driving but grounded for helicopters. Never push a pilot or glacier guide to carry on when they judge conditions unsafe.

For road trips, check [Alaska 511](https://511.alaska.gov/) and the forecast for the whole route as well as in downtown Anchorage. Carry emergency clothing and supplies in your car and plan to drive back in daylight if you can.

### What to wear for a winter glacier visit

Whatever you book, bring:

- Thermal base layers that move moisture away from your skin.
- An insulating mid-layer and a windproof, waterproof outer layer.
- Warm socks and insulated, supportive winter boots that meet the operator's rules.
- A warm hat and gloves, plus a spare pair of gloves.
- Sunglasses for bright snow and low winter sun.
- Water, snacks, any medication you need and a charged phone.
- A spare camera battery, because cold drains batteries fast.

On a guided walk, follow the operator's gear list exactly. Crampons don't replace a guide's route-finding, and ordinary city snow boots may not take the operator's ice equipment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are any glaciers near Anchorage open in November?

Yes. Year-round Knik Glacier helicopter landings and guided Matanuska Glacier walks run in November. Dedicated winter ice cave, snowshoe and climbing tours start later, so check the calendar of the tour you want, not just the operator's season.

### Can you walk on a glacier in Alaska in December?

Yes, with a qualified guide on a glacier such as Matanuska when conditions allow. A Knik helicopter tour also gives you a short stop on or beside the ice. Never cross a glacier without a guide.

### What's the closest glacier you can drive to from Anchorage?

Portage Valley's glacier scenery is the shorter drive, but Portage Glacier itself can no longer be seen from the Begich, Boggs Visitor Center deck. For a guided walk on glacier ice, Matanuska Glacier is the road-access option. The answer depends on whether you want distant scenery, a roadside view or a legal guided walk on the ice.

### Is Matanuska Glacier open all winter?

Guided tours run through the winter: 907 Tours sells its Matanuska trip year-round, and Greatland runs its tour in winter and spring. That doesn't mean the state recreation site's viewing area is open all winter, or that you can go onto the glacier without a guide. A guided trip uses an established access arrangement, subject to weather, road and ice conditions.

### Can I see Knik Glacier without a helicopter?

You can see parts of the Knik Glacier landscape from a few distant viewpoints, but reaching the glacier is not a simple roadside walk. A helicopter landing is the easy organized way to get close. Don't treat a mapped track, a riverbed or a frozen lake as a safe route on your own.

### Is it better to visit Knik or Matanuska Glacier in January?

Choose Knik for the helicopter ride, the mountain views and a shorter stop on the ground. Choose Matanuska for a long guided walk on the ice and a road trip. Both need winter clothing and some flexibility in your schedule.

### Can children visit a glacier in winter?

Often, but age minimums, equipment sizes, cold exposure and effort differ by tour. Greatland's Matanuska winter group tour takes children from age 8, and 907 Tours prices children 14 and younger at $259. Some helicopter sightseeing tours suit families within the aircraft and operator rules. Check the rules of the exact tour before you book.

### Do you have to book a helicopter to see blue glacier ice?

No. A guided Matanuska walk can reach blue-ice formations with no flight at all. A Knik helicopter tour can look beautiful even when snow hides most of the blue ice. Neither can guarantee a particular formation.

### Can you walk across the frozen lake to Portage Glacier?

Don't plan a winter lake crossing on your own. Ice conditions vary and the area has avalanche and terrain hazards. A route that turns up in winter trip reports is not proven safe for a casual visitor, so ask qualified local guides and land managers about current conditions.

### Can I fit a glacier visit into one day in Anchorage?

Yes. A Knik helicopter tour fits into half a day with the drive and check-in, while a Matanuska glacier hike takes a full day. Don't schedule either so tightly that a weather delay makes you miss an onward flight.

### What happens if snow or wind cancels a helicopter tour?

Cancellation, rebooking and refund rules vary by operator and booking site, so read the terms before you pay. Keep a spare day if the flight is one of the main reasons for your trip.
