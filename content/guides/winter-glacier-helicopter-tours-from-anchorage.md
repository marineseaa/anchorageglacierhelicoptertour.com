Yes, you can take a glacier helicopter tour near Anchorage in winter. The standard Knik Glacier flights run year-round, and from mid-December you can also book tours that don't exist in summer: guided ice-cave trips, guided glacier walks and winter ice climbing on frozen glacier faces.

<!--more-->

Before you pay, check which winter product you are buying. A one-hour flight with a glacier landing is a different day from a guided two-hour ice-cave outing. And a tour sold as an *Anchorage* helicopter adventure usually leaves from Palmer, 45 to 60 minutes' drive from the city. It doesn't leave from downtown Anchorage.

This guide covers November 2026 to February 2027, with the operators' published starting prices, what the landing looks like in snow and the questions to ask before you book. For the whole year month by month, see our [best time for an Anchorage glacier helicopter tour](/guides/best-time-for-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour/) guide.

## The six winter tours in detail

### 1. Knik Glacier Landing: the simplest winter option

**Best for:** couples, first-time visitors to Alaska and anyone who mainly wants to stand on glacier ice without giving most of the day to it.

Alaska Helicopter Tours sells the {{< gyg-link tour="147264" >}}Knik Glacier Landing{{< /gyg-link >}} as a year-round one-hour trip from Alaska Glacier Lodge, outside Palmer. You fly for about 30 minutes and make one glacier landing, with about 30 minutes out of the helicopter on the ice.

In winter, expect white mountains, snow over the glacier and low-angle light, without the open blue meltwater pools of summer. The pilot picks the exact landing spot on the day, according to the snow and the weather.

**Why choose it:** it gives you the helicopter flight and the glacier stop in the shortest booking, which matters when winter daylight is short or your budget is tight.

**What to check:** that your date shows a bookable departure, how long you will spend out of the helicopter, and what winter footwear or traction the operator supplies.

[Compare Knik Glacier helicopter tours on our site](/knik-glacier-helicopter-tours/), or read our [review of the Knik Glacier Landing](/guides/is-the-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-worth-it/).

### 2. Outbound Heli's Knik Glacier Explorer: more flying and a flexible landing

**Best for:** travelers who want more time in the air and are happy for the pilot to choose the winter landing spot.

Outbound Heli Adventures lists its {{< gyg-link tour="492083" >}}Knik Glacier Explorer{{< /gyg-link >}} as a year-round, 90-minute trip with about 40 minutes of flying plus a landing on or near the glacier. Outbound's own starting price is $459. On GetYourGuide, Outbound sells a 1.5-hour flight from Palmer for $520, rated 4.93 from 36 reviews; we have not confirmed that it is the identical product, so compare the inclusions.

Outbound spells out the winter catch. When snow depth makes a landing on the glacier itself unsuitable, the pilot may land at the glacier's terminus near a large ice wall, on the shore of a lake full of icebergs, or in a gorge with ice caves. That gives you a good winter stop most days, but it means the booking does not promise you a particular patch of glacier ice.

Outbound supplies microspikes or ice cleats for walking on the ice, and its tours leave from Palmer.

**Why choose it:** compared with the one-hour landing, you get about ten more minutes of flying for a similar price, which suits you if the views from the air matter as much as the stop.

**What to check:** whether the booking guarantees a landing, what happens when the planned site is unusable, and how the 90 minutes split between flying and time on the ground.

[See the operator's Knik Glacier Explorer details](https://outboundheli.com/knik-glacier-explorer/).

### 3. Grand Knik: three landings in one trip

**Best for:** photographers, returning visitors and anyone booking a helicopter trip mainly for the variety of landscapes.

Alaska Helicopter Tours sells {{< gyg-link tour="412333" >}}Grand Knik{{< /gyg-link >}} as a two-hour trip with about 45 minutes of flying and three remote landings, on glacier and alpine terrain. The operator's price starts at $749 per person; on GetYourGuide it is $762, rated 4.98 from 26 reviews.

You pay for more stops and more viewpoints here. You don't get more time at any one spot. Winter snow can change which landing sites are usable. Ask about the route and the operator's fallback plan before you book.

**Why choose it:** you want more than one view of the Knik River valley and the Chugach Mountains, and you can put a bigger share of the budget into the flight.

**What to check:** whether all three landings usually happen in winter and what replaces a site that is unsafe on the day.

[View the Grand Knik and other Knik tours on the operator's site](https://www.alaskahelicoptertours.com/glacier-tours/).

### 4. Winter Ice Cave Adventure: for blue-ice formations

**Best for:** travelers looking specifically for an Alaska ice cave, more than a scenic flight.

Outbound Heli Adventures runs a dedicated <a href="https://outboundheli.com/winter-ice-cave-adventure/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Winter Ice Cave Adventure</a> from December through March. The standard trip lasts two hours, starts at $599 per person and leaves from Outbound's base in Palmer. You fly into the Chugach Mountains and stop to explore glacier ice formations with a guide. Outbound also offers a second cave stop (Alaska.org lists that version at $724 for 2.5 hours) and a private upgrade for $350. Outbound sells this tour on its own website; it is not listed on GetYourGuide.

An ice cave is a natural formation that changes through the season. Local guides decide where you can go and whether it is safe to enter, so a tour may not show you the same blue tunnel as the photos, and no particular cave is promised.

**Why choose it:** in winter, a cave can be more memorable than a quick stop on a wide snowfield. If your main goal is to see the texture and color of glacier ice up close, this is the closest match.

**What to check:** that the product includes guided ice-cave exploration as well as a landing near an ice wall, and what the operator offers when cave access is unsafe.

If you would rather book on GetYourGuide, Outbound's {{< gyg-link tour="492083" >}}Palmer flight{{< /gyg-link >}} is the nearest option: in winter its landing alternatives include a gorge with ice caves, though the cave is not guaranteed.

### 5. Winter Glacier Exploration: a guided walk on the ice

**Best for:** visitors who want to spend the ground time walking with a guide instead of taking photos beside the helicopter.

Alaska Helicopter Tours' <a href="https://www.alaskahelicoptertours.com/winter-glacier-exploration/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Winter Glacier Exploration</a> is a two-hour guided trip with about 25 minutes of flying, sold from December 15 to March 31. On the glacier you walk between half a mile and two miles with a guide, on microspikes or snowshoes depending on the snow, with trekking poles and helmets when needed. Ice caves are sometimes part of the route but not always, and ice skating is added when conditions allow; mention it when you book if you are interested.

The product page lists it from $1,049 and does not say whether that is per person, while the operator's winter overview page still shows "from $799". Check the amount at checkout before you plan around either figure. This tour is sold through the operator, not on GetYourGuide.

**Why choose it:** you get more to do on the ground than on a sightseeing landing, at a moderate effort level that the operator rates suitable for all ages.

**What to check:** which activities are included on your date, what equipment is supplied and how much walking you are comfortable with.

### 6. Glacier ice climbing by helicopter: the premium winter adventure

## Which winter helicopter tour should you book?

Pick on what you want to do after the helicopter lands, more than on which glacier photo looks best.

- **You have never flown in a helicopter:** start with the {{< gyg-link tour="147264" >}}Knik Glacier Landing{{< /gyg-link >}}. It is short and gives you the mountain scenery and the glacier in one hour.
- **You want a longer flight without paying for several stops:** compare Outbound's {{< gyg-link tour="492083" >}}Knik Glacier Explorer{{< /gyg-link >}}.
- **Landscape photography matters most:** look at {{< gyg-link tour="412333" >}}Grand Knik{{< /gyg-link >}}, but confirm how the winter landings can change and allow for shifting light.
- **You want to see an ice cave:** book a dedicated ice-cave tour such as Outbound's Winter Ice Cave Adventure. A standard landing does not promise a cave.
- **You want an active guided day:** compare Winter Glacier Exploration and {{< gyg-link tour="412355" >}}Glacier Ice Climbing{{< /gyg-link >}} by your fitness, the equipment supplied and the activities on your date.
- **You're visiting in November:** look first at the year-round Knik landing flights. The dedicated cave, walking and climbing tours start in December.

## Can you take a glacier helicopter tour in every winter month?

### November: year-round landings, no specialist tours yet

November brings colder temperatures, snow and quickly shortening days. The year-round Knik flights still run, subject to weather and the dates on the calendar. The dedicated winter products start later: Outbound's ice-cave tour in December, and the guided glacier walk and ice climbing on December 15.

**How to plan it:** book a standard Knik landing or a multi-landing tour and keep your schedule flexible.

### December: ice-cave season begins, with the shortest days

Outbound's ice-cave tours start in December, and Alaska Helicopter Tours' Winter Glacier Exploration and ice climbing start on December 15. December also has the least daylight: on the December 21 solstice, Anchorage gets about 5 hours 28 minutes between sunrise (about 10:14 a.m.) and sunset (about 3:42 p.m.).

**How to plan it:** book a midday slot, plan your drive with care and don't schedule a helicopter trip right after a flight arrives.

### January: deep winter and ice formations

January suits ice caves and guided glacier walks well. Ice climbing also runs, booked on request, and the days are still short, which limits flight times.

**How to plan it:** pick a tour that is clearly offered on your date; much of the summer catalog, such as glacier dogsledding and paddleboarding, does not run in winter.

### February: more daylight, the same winter tours

By February, Anchorage gains daylight quickly while the landscape stays wintry. That makes it a good month for a glacier flight, an ice-cave trip or a guided walk. All three winter specialty tours are scheduled through February and March, with ice climbing still on request.

**How to plan it:** try an ice-cave tour for something you can only do in winter, and book the flight early in your stay to leave room for a weather delay.

## What does a glacier look like in winter?

The photos used to sell Alaska glacier trips often show turquoise lakes, bare blue ice, running waterfalls and summer dogsled camps. You won't see all of those in January.

In winter you see wide white snowfields, dark mountain walls, frozen waterfalls, rough glacier edges and blue ice where it is exposed. The sun stays low and lights the mountains from the side. Deep snow can also hide crevasses and change where guides can take you.

A winter glacier landing and a guided ice-cave tour are different products. A standard tour may stop beside an ice wall or at another safe spot, and it does not include going into a cave. Our guide to [Knik Glacier helicopter tours in winter](/guides/knik-glacier-helicopter-tours-in-winter/) goes through the landing options on the Knik in snow.

## Winter weather: will your helicopter tour fly?

A winter tour on sale does not mean the weather will cooperate. Alaska Helicopter Tours names strong wind, fog, low cloud and icing as the conditions that can delay or cancel a flight. Snow and high wind can also rule out a particular landing site.

Use these practical rules:

1. **Schedule the flight early in your Alaska stay**, not on the last morning before you go home.
2. **Keep a spare day free** if the helicopter trip is a main reason for your visit.
3. **Read the weather-cancellation and refund policy** for your exact product and booking platform.
4. **Give your correct weights** when you book. Seat and load limits are safety rules for the aircraft.
5. **Keep your phone on you on the day** and follow the operator's instructions on weather updates and check-in.

Nobody can promise months ahead that a given December morning will be flyable. If the glacier trip matters most, a flexible itinerary helps you more than guessing the perfect day.

## What should you wear for a winter glacier helicopter tour?

Even on a short flight, you may stand outside on a windy glacier or beside an exposed ice wall. Dress for standing still outdoors in an Alaska winter, which is colder than walking around Anchorage.

Pack or wear:

- Thermal base layers and a warm mid-layer.
- A windproof, insulated winter jacket.
- Warm pants or snow pants, ideally windproof.
- Waterproof, insulated boots with good grip.
- Warm socks, gloves or mittens, and a hat.
- Sunglasses for the glare off snow and ice.
- A small camera or phone with a spare battery or power bank; keep batteries warm, because the cold drains them.

Some operators provide microspikes, crampons or technical gear depending on the tour. Ordinary hiking shoes are not enough for a winter glacier stop, and a standard flight does not include winter clothing. Confirm what your booking needs.

For a fuller packing list, see our [guide to what to wear on an Alaska glacier helicopter tour](/guides/what-to-wear-on-an-alaska-glacier-helicopter-tour/).

## Can you go glacier dog sledding by helicopter in winter?

This is one of the easiest Alaska tours to misread. The helicopter flight to a dogsled camp on a glacier is a summer product: operators fly you up to the high snow that stays on the glacier when the valleys are warm.

Alaska Helicopter Tours describes its glacier dogsled camps as a summer operation and lists no winter dates; this site's [best-time guide](/guides/best-time-for-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour/) gives May 1 to August 31 for the camp near Palmer. On GetYourGuide, the {{< gyg-link tour="147273" >}}Helicopter and Glacier Dogsled Tour{{< /gyg-link >}} from the Knik River (rated 4.89 from 64 reviews) runs in that summer window. Winter dog mushing near Anchorage is a separate activity on land, away from the glacier camp.

If mushing is essential to your trip, book it as its own winter activity. A glacier helicopter landing in winter does not come with a dog team.

## Is a winter glacier helicopter tour worth it?

It can be, especially if you are in Alaska in winter anyway and want to see places you can't reach by car. The helicopter gives you views across the Knik River valley and the Chugach Mountains, and a landing lets you stand and walk on frozen country you would otherwise see only from a distance.

It is the wrong purchase if you need a guaranteed ice cave, perfect blue-ice photos, wildlife sightings or a departure that cannot move for weather. Those depend on nature, and a responsible operator won't promise them.

For a first winter flight, start with a standard Knik landing. Pay more for an ice cave, several stops or a guided climb only if that activity is the reason you are booking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are glacier helicopter tours available near Anchorage in December and January?

Yes. The Palmer operators fly Knik Glacier tours year-round, and the winter-only tours (ice caves, guided glacier walks and ice climbing) run in December and January. Check a live booking calendar for your date, because not every tour runs every day.

### Do helicopter tours take off from downtown Anchorage?

The Knik Glacier tours in this guide don't. They leave from bases near Palmer, about 45 to 60 minutes from Anchorage by road. Transfers can be arranged separately.

### Can you land on Knik Glacier in winter?

Often, yes, and the Knik landing tours run year-round. The pilot makes the final choice of landing site. Snow depth and surface conditions can mean landing beside the glacier, at an ice wall or a frozen lake, instead of on top of it.

### Can you visit Alaska ice caves in November?

Don't count on a dedicated ice-cave tour in November. Outbound's Winter Ice Cave Adventure runs from December to March. Some year-round flights stop near ice formations, but confirm a cave visit separately.

### How many hours of daylight does Anchorage get in December?

On the December 21 solstice, Anchorage gets about 5 hours 28 minutes of daylight, with sunrise around 10:14 a.m. and sunset around 3:42 p.m. Operators fit winter flights into those hours and the weather.

### Are winter glacier tours suitable for children?

Some standard glacier landings and the guided glacier walk are sold for all ages. Whether they suit your family depends on each child's comfort with the flight, the cold, uneven ground and any equipment rules. Check with the operator before booking, especially for ice caves and climbing.

### What if my helicopter tour is canceled because of snow or wind?

The policy depends on the operator and the booking platform. Alaska Helicopter Tours and Outbound both try to reschedule weather-canceled flights and refund you when they can't. Read your booking terms, because not every cancellation follows the same rules.

### Is February better than December for a winter glacier flight?

February has more daylight, which makes flights easier to schedule, and still has snowy scenery. December has fewer daylight hours for the operators to work with. Neither month promises better flying weather on your date.

## Before you book: five questions to ask

Ask the seller or operator:

1. **Is my winter date available**, and is the price shown the final per-person charge?
2. **Where exactly do we check in**, and is transport from Anchorage included?
3. **Does this product include a glacier landing, an ice-cave visit or a guided walk?** They are different tours.
4. **How much of the total time is flying**, and how long is the stop on the ground?
5. **What happens if weather changes the route, the landing site or the date?**
