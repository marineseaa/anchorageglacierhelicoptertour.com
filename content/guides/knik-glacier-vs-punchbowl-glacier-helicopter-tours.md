Anchorage has two helicopter-accessible glaciers, and they sit in opposite directions. Choosing between them is not really a choice between glaciers — it is a choice between bare ice and permanent snow, and the two give you completely different days.

## The short answer

**Knik** if you want to stand on bare, crevassed glacier ice beside turquoise meltwater pools, and if price or choice matters — six tours, from $520.

**Punchbowl** if you want summer dogsledding on a real snowfield, if you are travelling with children, or if your day is already pointing south — two tours, from $769.

## Where they are

**Knik Glacier** lies about an hour north of Anchorage, up the Old Glenn Highway into the Knik River valley near Palmer. It is a big valley glacier: roughly twenty-five miles long, over five miles across at its widest, and one of the largest in southcentral Alaska. Its meltwater feeds the Knik River, which runs another twenty-five miles down into Knik Arm.

**Punchbowl Glacier** sits at around 3,200 feet in the Chugach Mountains above Girdwood, which is 40 miles and about 45 minutes south of Anchorage on the Seward Highway. It is a snowfield rather than a broad valley glacier, and it holds snow all year.

## What you actually land on

This is the difference that matters, and it is not obvious from the listings.

**Knik gives you ice.** Through summer, meltwater collects in hollows and drains through the glacier, leaving pools of a colour that looks retouched in photographs. The surface is crevassed and rubbly in places, which is why operators hand out microspikes or overboots. Every guided walk, ice climb and paddleboard on this site happens here, because you need exposed ice and open water for all three.

**Punchbowl gives you snow.** A white, high, relatively smooth surface — which sounds like less until you understand why it exists. Snow at 3,200 feet lasts through July, and that is what makes the dog camp possible. You cannot run a sled team on bare crevassed ice.

So: Knik is where you go to look at a glacier. Punchbowl is where you go to do something on one.

## The weather argument

The operators flying Knik claim a better dispatch rate than glacier flights working out of Girdwood, Seward, Juneau, Haines or Skagway. That is a marketing claim on a booking listing rather than a published statistic, and we treat it as one — but the geography under it is real. Turnagain Arm draws coastal cloud up from Cook Inlet, and Girdwood sits on it. The Knik River valley is further inland, behind a ridge.

In practice this only decides the booking if your trip has a single flyable morning in it. If it does, we would go north. If you have two or three chances, book the tour you actually want and let the weather sort itself out — every tour here reschedules or refunds rather than flying into conditions the pilot does not like. The [best-time guide](/guides/best-time-for-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour/) covers the rest of the seasonal picture.

## What each one costs

Knik starts at **$520** for the [Palmer flight](/tours/from-palmer-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour/), which also gives the longest ice time on the site — forty-five minutes with microspikes. The [flagship landing tour](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landing/) is $522 for thirty minutes on the ice, and is the best-reviewed tour here at 4.99 from 85 guests. Everything from a [two-hour three-landing flight](/tours/anchorage-grand-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landings/) at $762 to [ice climbing](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-and-ice-climbing-tour/) at $1,416 sits above them.

Punchbowl starts at **$769** for the [dogsledding tour](/tours/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding-tour/), with the [90-minute two-landing flight](/tours/anchorage-90-min-flight-with-2-landings/) at $844. There is no cheap Girdwood option.

## If you can only do one

Do Knik. It has the ice, the melt pools, the price range and five of the seven kinds of tour on this site, and the [flagship landing flight](/knik-glacier-helicopter-tours/) is both the cheapest way onto a glacier here and the highest-rated experience on the whole site.

Go to Girdwood instead if you specifically want to mush a team on summer snow with children in tow, if you have already committed to a day down Turnagain Arm, or if a second landing on an alpine ridge is the photograph you came for. Both are covered on the [Girdwood page](/glacier-landing-tours-from-girdwood/) and the [dogsledding page](/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the closest glacier to Anchorage?

By drive time, Punchbowl above Girdwood — 40 miles and roughly 45 minutes south. Knik Glacier is about an hour north up the Old Glenn Highway. Neither is reachable without a drive, because no glacier helicopter tour departs from Anchorage itself.

### Which glacier has the blue ice?

Knik. It is a bare, crevassed valley glacier, and through summer meltwater collects on and drains through the surface, leaving turquoise pools. Punchbowl is a snowfield at 3,200 feet that stays white year-round, which is exactly why a dog team can live on it in July.

### Can you dog sled on Knik Glacier too?

Yes, but not on the glacier ice itself — the [Knik valley dogsled tour](/tours/anchorage-area-helicopter-and-glacier-dogsled-tour/) flies to a snowfield camp above the glacier and gives a full hour with the team, which is longer than the Girdwood tour's sled time. Both are real snow; both need a helicopter to reach.

### Is Punchbowl Glacier worth the higher price?

If you want summer dogsledding with gear provided and a shorter drive, yes. If you want to stand on glacier ice, no — Knik does that better and starts $249 cheaper. The two are different products rather than two prices for the same thing.

### Which one is better for photographs?

Knik for ice: crevasse fields, meltwater pools and the scale of a twenty-five-mile glacier. Girdwood for altitude: the 90-minute flight's second landing on an alpine ridge or a mountain lake gives you the view back across the icefield, which is a harder photograph to get anywhere else.
