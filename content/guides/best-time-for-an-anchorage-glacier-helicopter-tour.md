<p class="guide-answer__label">The short answer</p>
<p class="guide-answer__text">Glacier landing flights near Anchorage, including those to Knik Glacier, are available year-round, while dogsledding and paddleboarding on the glacier are summer-season tours. On the day, the weather comes first: the pilot needs good visibility, and low cloud, strong wind or icing can delay or cancel a flight.</p>
<p class="guide-answer__text">If your dates are flexible, mid-May to August is the best overall time for a first trip: all the summer glacier tours run and blue meltwater pools usually form on the ice. May and June are usually drier than July and August around Palmer, the town nearest the Knik River base.</p>
<p class="guide-answer__text">September brings Southcentral Alaska's peak fall color, although it is cooler, rain is about as frequent as in August, and the dogsled tours have finished. From October to April Alaska is quieter, and landing flights continue. From December to March the glacier is covered in snow and ice-cave tours are offered, but mid-December has only about 5 1/2 hours of daylight, which leaves fewer flight times.</p>
<p class="guide-answer__text">There is no universal best time of day: no hour is reliably clearer, so choose the time that suits your day. Once your Alaska dates are fixed, schedule the flight early in your stay: a cancelled flight then has more days to move to, if the operator still has seats. If your booking has free cancellation, check the forecast before its deadline: low cloud can hide the ice field even when a flight goes, and you can still change plans for free.</p>

## The glacier season by season

Knik Glacier is about an hour's drive northeast of Anchorage, and seven of the ten tours on this site leave from the Knik River valley near Palmer; the other three leave Girdwood, 45 minutes south. A landing tour takes about an hour, with roughly 30 minutes in the air and 30 on the ice, so the season changes what you see and which tours you can book more than how long the flight lasts.

### Mid-May to August: all the summer tours run

This is when the glacier shows its summer face. Blue meltwater pools open on the surface from late spring, and Alaska.org, in a post sponsored by Outbound Heli Adventures, puts them at mid-May through September. Glacier dogsledding runs from May 1 to August 31 at Alaska Helicopter Tours' camp at 3,200 feet, and from May 15 to August 30 with Alpine Air in Girdwood. Paddleboarding on a meltwater pool and summer ice climbing both run from May 15 to September 15.

Around Palmer, average highs reach 59°F in May, 66°F in June and 68°F in July, warm enough for a fleece and a windproof layer on the ice, which is colder than the valley. May and June are the drier months, with 0.62 and 1.00 inches of rain on average. Rain is more frequent from July, and August averages 2.32 inches over about 14 wet days. Mid-June has 19 hours 26 minutes of daylight at the Knik River, so in summer the light does not limit when you fly.

### September: fall color and the end of summer

Travel Alaska says Southcentral Alaska's fall colors peak in September. Paddleboarding and summer ice climbing run until September 15, but the dogsled camps have closed. It is about as wet as August around Palmer, with 2.08 inches of rain over about 13 wet days, and average highs fall to 56°F, so bring a warmer layer. Alaska Helicopter Tours sells fall for "the gorgeous colors of fall and thinner crowds than the other seasons."

### October and November: first snow, shorter days

Landing flights continue, but the summer activity tours have stopped. Snow returns in October, about 4 inches in an average month near Palmer, average highs drop from 42°F to 29°F, and daylight shrinks from about 10 hours in mid-October to 7 hours by mid-November. Outbound Heli Adventures says it usually offers ice-cave visits in place of a landing on the glacier surface from late fall.

### December to March: snow and ice caves

The glacier is white, and this is the season for the tours you cannot do in summer. Outbound runs its Winter Ice Cave Adventure from December to March, and Alaska Helicopter Tours runs winter ice climbing from December 15 to March 31. Ice caves are natural formations that change from year to year, so no particular cave is promised. Average highs at Palmer are 22 to 34°F, and you need full winter clothing.

Daylight is the main limit. Mid-December has 5 hours 25 minutes between sunrise and sunset at the Knik River, and Alaska Helicopter Tours flies only in daylight in winter, so early-morning and late-afternoon departures are not offered. By mid-March the days are back to almost 12 hours. The northern lights are possible on winter nights, but the flights run in daylight.

### April to mid-May: spring snow and long days

April is the driest month at Palmer, with 0.30 inches of rain, and the days lengthen fast, to almost 15 hours by mid-April. The glacier is still covered in snow, and the summer activity tours have not started. April and May also had the most gusty hours at Palmer airport, and strong gusts can cancel a flight on the day.

## How the weather affects a helicopter flight

A helicopter tour runs on visual flying, so the pilot needs to see. Alpine Air lists "high winds, fog, low clouds or icing conditions" as the reasons flights are delayed or cancelled. Alaska Helicopter Tours says its pilots are legal to fly down to half a mile of visibility but may reschedule when it is low, and it stops for winds over 30 knots or gusts of 15 knots or more above the steady wind. Low cloud matters twice: it can stop the flight, and it can hide the ice field and the peaks you came to see.

This happens in every month. To see how often, we counted the daytime hours at Palmer airport from 2016 to 2025 with a cloud base of at least 3,000 feet and at least 5 miles of visibility. April to June came out highest, at 93 to 96%, July at 88%, and November to February lowest, at 81 to 84%, when low cloud or poor visibility was most frequent. Palmer is about 20 miles from the glacier, and the mountains around it can hold cloud when the airport is clear, so read these as a guide to the season, not a promise about a day. April and May had the most hours with gusts of 25 knots or more.

Girdwood is a much wetter place to fly from. It averages 70.9 inches of precipitation a year, more than five times Palmer's 12.6 inches, and September to December are its wettest months. If your trip has a single free day, the Knik River flights leave from the drier valley.

If a flight cannot go, the operators reschedule it or refund you. Alaska Helicopter Tours aims to move you later the same day or to another date and refunds in full if it cancels for weather; Outbound looks at the weather on the day and contacts you; Alpine Air offers a later time or a full refund. All three advise booking at the start of your stay so there is time to rebook.

## The best time of day to fly

There is no time of day that is reliably clearer. Asked whether to choose a morning or afternoon tour, Alaska Helicopter Tours says "it's really your preference that matters most here," and notes that afternoon temperatures "tend to be a bit higher." Outbound says the best time "all depends on what you are hoping to experience," with photographers choosing softer light and wildlife more active at some hours. The airport record agrees: at Palmer, afternoon hours were as clear as morning ones, or slightly clearer, in every month.

So choose the time that fits your day. A morning flight leaves the afternoon for the drive back or a stop in Palmer; an afternoon flight means a slower start and a little more warmth on the ice. In winter the choice narrows to the daylight hours, and Outbound, which flies from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., notes that sunset flights are only possible in winter. ## How busy Alaska gets

About 88% of the people who visited Alaska in 2024–25 came in the summer season, from May to September, and fewer than 400,000 came between October and April, out of just over 3 million for the year. That is a count of visitors to the whole state, not of helicopter passengers, and none of the three operators we checked publishes passenger numbers by month. If you are traveling in summer with fixed dates, book ahead.

- Alaska Helicopter Tours publishes no dates for ice caves; Outbound's run from December to March. Ice caves change from year to year.
- The visitor figures cover all of Alaska for one year (2024–25) and were read second-hand; the state's own page could not be opened. They are a proxy for how busy Alaska is, not a count of helicopter passengers.
- There is no month-by-month price series for these tours, so the page says nothing about cheaper months.
