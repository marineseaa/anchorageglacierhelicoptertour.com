## Winter helicopter dog sledding vs summer glacier dog sledding

Picture "Alaska helicopter dog sledding" and you probably see a helicopter landing on a snowfield and dropping you at a camp where sled dogs live high on the ice. That tour is real, and it runs in summer.

In the warmer months the low trails have no snow, so operators fly guests up to a glacier snowfield where the dogs can still pull sleds. Alaska Helicopter Tours publishes May 1 to August 31 for its Anchorage-area glacier dog-sled tours, and Alpine Air's Punchbowl Glacier camp above Girdwood is a summer tour too. Our [summer helicopter glacier dogsledding page](/anchorage-helicopter-glacier-dogsledding/) compares those camps.

Winter turns it around. Once snow covers the ground trails around Willow and Big Lake, mushing moves down to the kennels. A helicopter can fly you to the kennel or add a scenic leg, but the sled ride happens on the winter trail system, not on Knik Glacier.

### Three tours that sound alike

**1. Helicopter to a glacier dog camp.** You land at a seasonal dog camp on a glacier and ride on the high snowfield. The Anchorage-area versions run in summer.

**2. Helicopter to a winter mushing kennel.** You fly over the Mat-Su valleys, land near a kennel or trailhead, meet the dogs and ride a snow trail. This is what a winter helicopter dog-sled package means.

**3. Helicopter, mushing and a glacier landing.** You visit the dogs and make a second helicopter stop on glacier ice. These are two separate parts of the day, and the dogs do not come to the glacier.

Outbound Heli Adventures says this plainly in its FAQ: it does not currently offer dog sledding on a glacier, and a glacier landing can be added to the dog-sled trip. Its winter tour is a kennel visit by air, sold as one.

Before you book for December, January or February, ask the operator one question: *"Will the dogs be on a glacier, or will the helicopter take us to a separate kennel and trail?"* The answer tells you what your day will look like.

## What winter dog sledding is like

A winter mushing trip is more than a passenger ride behind huskies. At many kennels you see how the dogs are cared for, trained and handled. Depending on the tour, you meet dogs one by one, watch them being harnessed, hear stories from working mushers and learn how a team responds to commands.

Dog sledding was everyday transport in Alaska long before it was a tourist ride. Some rides are gentle introductions, others let you take part. The word "mush" in a tour name does not mean you will drive the sled. Ask whether you ride as a passenger, stand on the runners beside a guide, or drive your own team under instruction.

### Will there be snow?

In midwinter the Willow and Mat-Su trails usually have enough snow for sleds, but early November and the end of the season are not certain. Conditions also differ between Anchorage, Palmer and Willow. An operator may change the route, use a wheeled cart, reschedule or cancel when the trail is not fit.

If a sled on snow is essential, ask for confirmation shortly before you travel. December to February is a safer bet than early November, without a guarantee.

### Will I see Denali from the helicopter?

Some operators mention possible Denali views, depending on route and weather. In winter, visibility changes fast and cloud can hide the mountain. Count a Denali view, and any wildlife, as a bonus; neither is part of what you paid for.

### How long will I spend with the dogs?

Read the wording closely. A 2.5-hour helicopter package includes the flight, the landing, safety briefings and the kennel time; it is not a 2.5-hour sled ride. Outbound says you spend more than an hour with the dog team on its basic trip. Alaskan Husky Adventures separates its 1.5-hour visit from 45 to 60 minutes of riding.

If time with the dogs matters most, compare the length of the sled ride and ignore the total tour time.

## November vs December vs January vs February: which month to choose

Daylight, snow cover and holiday demand change month by month. Our [best time for a glacier helicopter tour guide](/guides/best-time-for-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour/) covers the full year.

### November: early winter, thinner trails

November brings snowy mountain views, but the winter trails may still be building. If snowfall has been light, some tours use carts instead of sleds. Daylight shrinks fast through the month, so choose a start time with light for the whole trip.

**Plan:** ask whether traditional sleds are running on your date and what replaces them if the snow is too thin. Outbound's year-round trip may still run; what pulls you is the question.

### December: winter scenery, very short days

December is a beautiful month to see dog teams on snowy trails, but daylight around Anchorage is very short near the winter solstice. A helicopter-and-kennel trip may need to run in the middle of the day. Holiday travel makes early booking and checking operating days more important.

**Plan:** keep the whole daylight window free, do not book a tight flight connection afterward, and ask whether the Christmas-week or New Year schedule differs from the usual one.

### January: midwinter sledding and glacier packages

January is the month for the classic picture of sled dogs running over snow. Outbound's Dog Mushing & Glaciers season restarts on January 1. Flight weather, deep cold and winter roads can still upset the day.

**Plan:** keep a second day free in case the flight moves, and bring insulated boots and warm mittens.

### February: longer days and race season

Days get longer through February, which helps photos and midday sightseeing. Alaska's racing season is in full swing, and February is a lively month to meet a working team. A tourist kennel visit is not a race event, so do not plan it as a race day.

**Plan:** book the helicopter part early in your Anchorage stay. If you want photos of the dogs running, ask when the sled ride happens relative to the flight and whether there are photo stops.

## What to wear for a winter helicopter dog-sled tour

Dress for two settings: the short, sometimes exposed moments boarding the helicopter, and the time outdoors at the kennel and on the trail. Sitting still behind running dogs feels colder than walking at the same temperature, because of the wind and because you are not moving.

What to wear:

- Moisture-wicking base layers, with no cotton next to the skin.
- A fleece or insulated mid-layer and an insulated winter jacket.
- Warm, windproof snow pants or ski pants.
- Insulated winter boots with good grip and room for warm socks.
- Mittens or insulated gloves, plus thin liner gloves for photos.
- A warm hat or close-fitting head cover that works with the aircraft briefing.
- A neck gaiter for the ride, worn as the guide tells you.
- Sunglasses for the snow glare and a secure strap or pocket for your phone.
- Spare camera batteries, which drain faster in the cold.

Ask the operator whether it lends overboots, snowsuits, helmets or eye protection. Snowhook lists winter clothing on its ground tours; Outbound asks you to bring warm clothes. One company's gear policy does not apply to another. Our [glacier helicopter tour clothing guide](/guides/what-to-wear-on-an-alaska-glacier-helicopter-tour/) has the full layering list.

### Phones and cameras

Keep spare batteries close to your body, use wrist straps, and do not drop small accessories near working dogs. Photograph the team only when the guide says it is safe. Never step into the sled's path or distract the dogs while they are harnessed or setting off.

In the helicopter, follow the rules on loose items and seat belts. Never lean out, open a door or use an unsecured camera rig unless the operator has approved it for that aircraft and flight.

## Safety and dog-care questions to ask

A good operator can explain both how it keeps passengers safe and how it looks after the dogs.

For your own safety, follow the pilot's briefing and the musher's instructions. Stay clear of the rotor blades, wait to be led to the aircraft, never stand in front of an excited dog team and never reach into the harnesses without permission. Winter trails are slippery, and the dogs can take off fast once released.

If dog welfare matters to you, ask how the dogs are housed, exercised and rested, what vet care they get, how their workload changes with temperature and trail conditions, and whether older or retired dogs stay at the kennel. An operator that talks openly about daily care gives you more to base a booking on.

Kennels are not all run the same way, and mushing is hard work for the dogs. Choose an operator that answers your practical questions openly.

## What happens if weather cancels the helicopter?

Light snow does not stop a helicopter on its own, and a tight schedule will not make it fly. Pilots look at visibility, wind, icing, cloud, the landing area and the conditions along the route. The weather can be fine in Anchorage and unflyable near Palmer, Big Lake, Willow or the mountains.

Outbound says that if it cancels for weather, guests get a refund or a new date. Terms differ by operator, by package and by who cancels.

### How to plan around the weather

- Book the helicopter tour early in your Anchorage stay and avoid your last morning before flying home.
- Keep at least one spare day to reschedule.
- Ask whether the dog-sled part can go ahead without the helicopter if the flight is grounded, and whether you can choose that.
- Ask whether a weather cancellation refunds the whole package or each part separately.
- Hold off on nonrefundable transport or hotels until you know the operator's terms.
- Keep the day flexible, because changing conditions can move the departure time even when the tour runs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you do helicopter dog sledding in Alaska in December?

Yes, through winter helicopter-and-mushing packages. Outbound sells its basic trip year-round and its mushing-and-glacier package through December 31, and Snowhook sells a Winter Fly & Ride. Confirm a December departure, the trail conditions and the flight weather for your date.

### Can you go dog sledding by helicopter in January?

Yes. January falls inside Outbound's published season for both mushing trips, and Snowhook sells its Fly & Ride as a winter tour. January is also a good month for road-access sled rides. Check the exact date before booking.

### What about February helicopter dog sledding from Anchorage?

February is a good month to look at winter helicopter-and-sled packages. Longer days later in the month help with photos and scheduling, though flight weather stays changeable.

### Can you dog sled on Knik Glacier in winter?

No. Outbound says it does not currently offer dog sledding on a glacier; its package puts the sled ride at a separate kennel and adds a Knik Glacier landing. Alaska Helicopter Tours runs its glacier-camp dogsledding from May to August.

### Is a dog-sled tour with a glacier landing worth the extra money?

It is if you want to meet sled dogs and step onto a glacier on the same trip. If you only want to try dog sledding, a road-access kennel tour costs far less. If you only want the glacier, a standard helicopter landing is simpler.

### Does a winter helicopter dog-sled tour depart from downtown Anchorage?

Not usually. Outbound starts from Palmer and can help arrange transport from Anchorage. Snowhook's Winter Fly & Ride lists an Anchorage airport departure; confirm the airport and your transfers. "Anchorage" in a tour name does not tell you where the helicopter takes off.

### How much is a winter helicopter dog-sled tour near Anchorage?

Published prices start at $699 for Outbound's basic mushing flight, $799 per adult for Snowhook's Winter Fly & Ride and $999 for Outbound's mushing-plus-glacier package. These are starting prices, and none is a quote for your dates.

### Can children go helicopter dog sledding?

Some packages are sold as family-friendly, but age rules, seat requirements, weight limits and sled arrangements differ. Confirm them with the operator, especially for infants and very young children.

### Will I get to drive the sled myself?

Maybe, but not every tour includes it. A kennel may offer a passenger ride, supervised help or a separate drive-your-own-team trip. Ask how much hands-on mushing is included before you pay.

### What if there isn't enough snow for a sled?

Early in the season or in a poor snow year, a tour may use a wheeled cart or a different trail, or reschedule or cancel. If a sled on snow is essential, ask the operator what it will run on your date and what happens if the snow is too thin.

### Does a helicopter dog-sled ride include the northern lights?

No. These packages run in daylight. Aurora viewing needs dark, clear skies, so plan it as a separate evening outing.

### Is a helicopter tour safer or more reliable than driving to a kennel in winter?

They carry different risks. Helicopters depend on the weather and the pilot's call; winter road trips depend on the driving conditions, the vehicle and the driver. A ground tour may run when a flight cannot, but neither is guaranteed to go ahead.

### Should I book the helicopter and dog sledding separately?

Consider it if you want price flexibility, more time with the dogs, or a glacier flight on a day you can move for weather. A combined package is easier to arrange if you want both and are happy to pay for the convenience.
