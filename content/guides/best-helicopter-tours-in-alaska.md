"Helicopter tour" can mean very different things in Alaska. One flight gives you scenery and a return to the helipad. Another lands you directly on the ice. Others use the helicopter purely as transport to reach a glacier for dog sledding, hiking or ice climbing — and the prices swing from $520 to well over $1,400 depending on which one you book.

<!--more-->

For most first-time visitors, **a helicopter flight with a glacier landing offers the best balance of scenery, experience and cost.** You get the aerial views that make helicopter sightseeing special, and you also get out of the aircraft and stand on the glacier itself. That isn't necessarily the right choice for everyone — if the flight is the main attraction, a longer multi-landing route can be better; if you want something more memorable than sightseeing alone, dog sledding or heli-hiking turns the ride into a bigger adventure.

Below, we compare the main types of helicopter tour available in Alaska, what you're actually paying for, and which real, bookable tours near Anchorage deliver each one.

## 1. Glacier Landing Helicopter Tour — Best Overall

If you've never taken a helicopter tour in Alaska before, this is where we'd start.

A **glacier landing helicopter tour** combines the two things most people actually want: seeing Alaska's mountains and glaciers from the air, and stepping onto a glacier. The flight gives you the scale — how the ice stretches through entire valleys, where it meets the surrounding mountains, how meltwater rivers flow away from it. Then the helicopter lands, and the landscape you were looking down on becomes something you're standing inside.

### What usually happens?

A typical experience includes a safety briefing, a scenic flight toward the glacier, views over mountains and glacial valleys, a landing on or near the ice, time outside the aircraft, and a return scenic flight. It isn't normally a strenuous hike — you stay relatively close to the helicopter and follow the pilot or guide's instructions, which makes a basic glacier landing accessible to a much wider range of travellers than trekking or ice climbing.

### Who should choose it?

Particularly good if it's your first Alaska helicopter tour, you have limited time, you want to actually stand on a glacier, you don't want a physically demanding activity, or you want a strong balance between flying and exploring.

### Potential downside

Part of the advertised tour duration goes to the glacier stop rather than the air. If your priority is seeing as much of Alaska from the air as possible, a longer scenic flight may suit you better.

## 2. Multi-Glacier Scenic Helicopter Tour — Best for the Views

Some people don't particularly care about walking around on the ice. They want to fly.

If that's you, look for a **longer, multi-landing helicopter tour**. Instead of concentrating on a single glacier stop, these flights use more of the available time to explore the landscape from above and put you down in more than one spot. Depending on the route, you may pass multiple glacier faces, mountain ridges, icefields, waterfalls, glacier-fed rivers and remote sections of the Chugach Mountains. The important thing is to compare **actual flight time**, not simply total tour duration.

## 3. Helicopter + Glacier Dog Sledding — Best Bucket-List Experience

This is one of the most distinctive ways to combine two classic Alaska experiences. You fly by helicopter into snow-covered glacier terrain and meet a working sled-dog team before heading out onto the snow — a single combined experience rather than two unrelated activities booked separately.

### Why is the helicopter important?

Because of altitude. The helicopter can reach glacier terrain where snow conditions stay suitable even when the valleys below are green, which is why summer dog sledding on real snow is possible at all near Anchorage.

### Who should choose it?

Particularly appealing for families, first-time Alaska visitors, couples, people celebrating an occasion, or travellers with a big bucket list who would otherwise book both activities separately.

## 4. Heli-Hiking or Glacier Trekking — Best for Active Travellers

A standard glacier landing lets you stand on the ice. A heli-hiking or trekking tour lets you actually **explore it**. The helicopter provides access to terrain that would otherwise be difficult or time-consuming to reach on foot, and the guided portion becomes the main activity — glacier features, ridges, meltwater channels, blue ice and moraines, viewed with a guide rather than photographed from a fixed spot near the aircraft.

### Glacier landing vs. heli-hiking

A standard landing puts you down, gives you time near the helicopter to look around and photograph, and flies you back. Heli-hiking makes the landing the beginning rather than the whole experience — a guide takes you farther through the terrain, and glacier exploration becomes a substantial part of the tour.

## 6. Winter Glacier Helicopter Tour — Best for an Off-Season Alaska Trip

Alaska helicopter sightseeing isn't exclusively a summer activity. Winter creates an entirely different landscape: snow-covered mountains, frozen valleys, white glacier surfaces, lower light and dramatic shadows across the Chugach.

**Advantages of winter:** distinctive snow-covered scenery, fewer summer tourists, a different atmosphere from peak-season Alaska, and — near Anchorage specifically — the chance to add ice cave exploration to the [ice-climbing tour](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-and-ice-climbing-tour/), only possible once meltwater channels have frozen solid.

**Downsides:** shorter daylight hours, colder conditions, fewer tourist activities overall, tour availability varying by operator, and weather disrupting plans more readily than in summer.

No single tour on this site is sold specifically as a "winter" product — winter changes what a handful of the existing tours look like rather than adding a new one. Our [best-time guide](/guides/best-time-for-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour/) goes through what actually changes month by month.

### Verdict

**A strong option for winter visitors — and proof that glacier helicopter sightseeing doesn't have to be a summer-only experience.**

## 7. Private Helicopter Tour — Best for Couples and Special Occasions

A private helicopter experience sounds appealing, but check carefully what "private" actually means. Booking two seats on a helicopter tour doesn't automatically mean the aircraft is reserved exclusively for you — many sightseeing flights combine passengers from different bookings.

If privacy matters — an anniversary, proposal, wedding or other occasion — check whether you're booking two seats on a shared departure or the entire helicopter.

**Worth paying for if:** privacy is central to the experience, you're planning a proposal, you're travelling as a small group, or photography is particularly important to you.

## Where Are the Best Places for Helicopter Tours in Alaska?

For most visitors, four regions stand out.

### Anchorage & Knik Glacier

**Best for:** easy glacier access from an Anchorage-based trip.

The Anchorage region is one of the easiest places to add a glacier helicopter tour without reorganising your whole itinerary. The Chugach Mountains surround the city, and **Knik Glacier** — around 25 miles long and over five miles across — is one of the major helicopter destinations in southcentral Alaska. See every [Knik Glacier tour on this site](/knik-glacier-helicopter-tours/).

### Girdwood & the Chugach Mountains

**Best for:** combining helicopter sightseeing with a scenic day south of Anchorage.

Girdwood lies 40 miles south and is surrounded by dramatic Chugach scenery, fitting well into an itinerary that also includes Turnagain Arm, Alyeska, Portage and onward travel toward the Kenai Peninsula. See the [Girdwood glacier landing tours](/glacier-landing-tours-from-girdwood/).

### Juneau & the Juneau Icefield

**Best for:** cruise passengers and guided glacier activities.

Juneau is another major centre for helicopter glacier experiences. The Juneau Icefield provides direct access to enormous glacier terrain, and current offerings there include glacier walks, trekking, ice climbing and dog sledding — none of which are sold through this site, which focuses specifically on the Anchorage-area operators above.

### Denali & Talkeetna

**Best for:** huge mountain scenery and Denali flightseeing.

Denali is a different kind of aerial experience, focused less on a short flight to a nearby glacier and more on the sheer scale of the Alaska Range. Many of the best-known Denali flightseeing tours use fixed-wing aircraft rather than helicopters.

## Which Alaska Helicopter Tour Gives You the Best Value?

The cheapest tour isn't necessarily the best value. Neither is the longest. Ask yourself what you're actually trying to experience.

**If you mainly want to ride in a helicopter:** book the shortest, cheapest landing tour rather than a longer activity tour — the [flagship](/tours/anchorage-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landing/) or [Palmer](/tours/from-palmer-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour/) flights, both around $520.

**If you mainly want to experience a glacier:** book a glacier landing — the Palmer tour gives the most ice time on this site at forty-five minutes.

**If you mainly want aerial scenery:** pay for more flight time, not more time on the ground — the [Grand Knik tour](/tours/anchorage-grand-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landings/) or the [90-minute Girdwood flight](/tours/anchorage-90-min-flight-with-2-landings/).

**If dog sledding is already on your bucket list:** a combined helicopter + dog sledding tour can make sense despite the higher price.

**If you enjoy active adventures:** choose heli-hiking or ice climbing.

**If you're celebrating something special:** consider a smaller-group flight, but confirm with the operator whether you're genuinely getting exclusive use of the helicopter.

## Glacier Landing vs. Dog Sledding: Which Should You Choose?

**Choose the glacier landing if:** budget matters, you mainly want the helicopter experience, standing on a glacier is enough, you want a shorter tour, or you're already doing dog sledding elsewhere.

**Choose dog sledding if:** it's a major Alaska bucket-list item, you want a longer experience, you're travelling with children who would love it, or you're comfortable spending significantly more — $769 to $816 near Anchorage, against $520 to $522 for a straight landing.

## Short Helicopter Tour vs. Longer Flight

A longer flight sounds automatically better. It isn't always.

Longer flights become worthwhile when they genuinely add another glacier system, dramatically different terrain, an additional landing, or more time deep within the mountains — which is exactly what the [Grand Knik tour](/tours/anchorage-grand-knik-glacier-helicopter-tour-with-landings/)'s three touchdowns deliver over the flagship tour's one. Before upgrading, ask: **what exactly do I see on the longer tour that I don't see on the shorter one?**

## What to Look for Before Booking

1. **Actual flight time** — probably the most overlooked detail.
2. **Glacier landing** — never assume the word "glacier" means the helicopter lands.
3. **Number of landings** — longer tours may include more than one.
4. **Departure location** — an "Anchorage tour" doesn't mean a downtown Anchorage departure; every tour near Anchorage lifts off from Palmer or Girdwood.
5. **Transfers** — if the heliport is outside Anchorage, check whether hotel pickup or a shuttle is available; most tours here don't include one.
6. **Total duration vs. activity duration** — especially important for dog sledding, hiking and ice climbing.
7. **Weight policy** — helicopters operate under strict weight-and-balance requirements; see our [cost guide](/guides/how-much-does-an-anchorage-glacier-helicopter-tour-cost/) for the actual published limits and surcharges.
8. **Cancellation policy** — Alaska weather matters, and windows vary between 24 hours and 7 days depending on the operator.
